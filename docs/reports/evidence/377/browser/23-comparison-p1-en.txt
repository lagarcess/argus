PLTR vs LMT, Head-to-Head

Short answer: These are two very different bets in the "defense" world. Lockheed Martin (LMT) is the mature, hardware-heavy prime contractor, ~$77B in revenue, pays a big dividend, trades at a modest P/E of 22. Palantir (PLTR) is a fast-growing AI/data-software company, much smaller ($6B revenue) but far more profitable per dollar of sales, no dividend, and trades at an extreme P/E of ~146. The market values Palantir at nearly 3× Lockheed's market cap despite Lockheed generating ~12× the revenue.

What each company does
PLTR, Builds data/AI software platforms (Gotham for government/defense/intel, Foundry for commercial enterprises, AIP for large language model deployment). Asset-light, software margins.
LMT, Makes the physical hardware of national defense: F-35 fighter jets, missiles, helicopters (Sikorsky), radars, satellites. Four segments: Aeronautics, Missiles & Fire Control, Rotary & Mission Systems, Space. The U.S. government is by far its largest customer.
How to think about the comparison
Valuation: LMT trades like a mature industrial (~22× earnings); PLTR trades like a hyper-growth software story (~146× earnings). PLTR's price already reflects very high future growth expectations.
Cash return to shareholders: LMT returned ~$3.2B in dividends over the last twelve months; PLTR returns none.
Balance sheet: PLTR has $9.4B in cash, more than 2× LMT's cash pile, despite being far smaller. Total debt figures were not returned by the data source for either company, so I can't state leverage precisely here.
Scale vs. growth: LMT is ~12× larger by revenue; PLTR is growing ~10× faster.

Not investment advice, just the numbers side-by-side.
