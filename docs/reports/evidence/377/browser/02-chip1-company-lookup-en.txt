Netflix Revenue Streams Explained
How does Netflix make money?
Netflix mainly makes money by charging people for access to its streaming service. It also sells advertising on its lower-priced ad-supported plans.
The revenue model
SOURCE	HOW NETFLIX EARNS MONEY
Subscriptions	Members pay a recurring monthly fee. Revenue grows when Netflix adds memberships, raises prices, or customers choose more expensive plans. Fees for “extra members” outside a household are also subscription revenue.
Advertising	Advertisers pay to place commercials in programming watched by members on the ad-supported plan. Netflix benefits from both the member’s subscription fee and the advertising shown to that member.
Other initiatives	Live events and games primarily make the subscription more attractive, improve retention, generate sign-ups, and, where applicable, create more advertising inventory. They are not currently described as major standalone revenue sources.

Netflix’s core economic loop is straightforward:

Spend money producing and licensing films, series, live programming, and games.
Use that content to attract and retain members.
Monetize the audience through subscription fees and advertising.
Keep the difference after content, technology, marketing, staffing, and other expenses.
How large is the business?

For the year ended December 31, 2025, Netflix reported:

Revenue: $45,183,036,000
Operating profit: $13,326,603,000
Net income attributable to common shareholders: $10,981,201,000
Operating cash flow: $10,149,273,000

Netflix operates globally. Its 2025 revenue by region was:

REGION	2025 REVENUE
United States and Canada	$19,957,152,000
Europe, Middle East and Africa	$14,514,646,000
Latin America	$5,357,521,000
Asia-Pacific	$5,353,717,000
Advertising is becoming more important

Subscriptions remain the main business, but advertising is growing quickly. As of Netflix’s April 16, 2026 earnings call, management expected 2026 advertising revenue to reach $3 billion. That was a company target, not a completed full-year result.

Live sports and entertainment events can support both sides of the model: they attract new members and provide valuable advertising opportunities. Games are still a small investment relative to Netflix’s overall content spending and are currently intended mainly to increase the value and use of the membership rather than generate substantial direct sales.

Bottom line: Netflix sells recurring entertainment access. Subscriptions provide the bulk of its money, while advertising is its increasingly significant second revenue engine.
