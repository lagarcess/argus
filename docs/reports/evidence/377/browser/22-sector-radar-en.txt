Cybersecurity Stocks Surge Driven by AI
What's happening in cybersecurity stocks?
Cybersecurity stocks are surging, but the rally is sharply divided

As of the latest close, Friday, August 7, 2026, cybersecurity ETFs are near 52-week highs and have substantially outperformed the broader U.S. market in 2026. Investors are treating cybersecurity as a major beneficiary of AI: more AI agents and automated systems create more identities, data and attack surfaces that need protection.

The catch is that execution matters enormously. Companies showing accelerating recurring revenue, strong margins or credible AI-security demand are soaring; those with slowing growth or weaker cash-flow guidance are being punished even after beating earnings estimates.

The leaders
Fortinet (FTNT) is the clearest 2026 momentum leader. Its July-quarter revenue was $2.0479 billion, beating consensus by $159.424 million; EPS was $0.90 versus $0.75 expected. Its preceding quarter produced a 20.03% one-day post-earnings gain.
Palo Alto Networks (PANW) is winning the consolidation story: customers increasingly want fewer vendors and broader integrated platforms. Fiscal Q3 revenue grew 31% to $3.0 billion, while next-generation security ARR rose 60% to $8.1 billion. The stock gained 11.4% after that report.
Okta (OKTA) became the standout identity-security name after management cited strong demand for protecting AI-agent identities. Its shares gained nearly 48% over two sessions following fiscal Q1 results.
CrowdStrike (CRWD) remains a major ETF driver. It is CIBR’s second-largest position at 8.56%, behind PANW at 9.42%. The stock initially fell 7% after its latest report because expectations were already extremely high, despite management raising net-new ARR growth guidance to 27.7%.

Among a broader interpretation of cybersecurity, Fastly (FSLY) was the strongest one-year performer in the Nasdaq CTA Cybersecurity Index, up 259.28% through August 3. That figure should not be confused with a pure-play large-cap comparison: Fastly is primarily an edge-cloud and application-delivery company.

The laggards
Zscaler (ZS) is the obvious large-cap laggard. It fell more than 31% following its latest earnings reaction, despite beating and raising guidance, because investors focused on decelerating ARR growth and a reduced free-cash-flow-margin outlook. Revenue still grew 25.4% to $850.5 million, illustrating how demanding valuations and expectations have become.
SentinelOne (S) had also been under pressure after quarterly revenue of $276.7 million, up 20.8%, came with weaker-than-expected billings and next-quarter EPS guidance. It subsequently recovered to $21.40 on August 7, only $0.10 below its 52-week high, so a reliable current YTD return is needed before calling it a continuing price laggard.
