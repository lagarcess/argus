Costco vs. Walmart vs. Target, the short answer

Walmart is the giant by revenue, Costco is the market's favorite (highest valuation multiple by far), and Target is the smaller, cheaper turnaround story. All three sell general merchandise and groceries at a discount, but their business models, growth, and how the market prices them differ sharply.

What actually makes each one different
Costco (COST), Membership-warehouse model. Sells fewer SKUs in bulk at razor-thin gross margins (~13%), but earns high-margin, recurring membership fees that drop almost entirely to the bottom line. Investors reward it with the richest multiple of the three (P/E ~48) because of consistent traffic, member loyalty, and steady comp-sales growth. CEO: Ron Vachris.
Walmart (WMT), The behemoth. Nearly 2.5× Costco's revenue and about 6.8× Target's. Broadest format mix (supercenters, Neighborhood Market, Sam's Club, Flipkart, walmart.com). Big pushes into e-commerce, advertising, and marketplace/fulfillment are why the market has re-rated it higher (P/E ~39) than it used to trade. CEO: John Furner.
Target (TGT), Smallest and cheapest on a P/E basis (~20). Focus on discretionary categories (apparel, home, décor via owned brands like Cat & Jack, Threshold, Good & Gather) makes it more sensitive to consumer swings than the other two. Highest dividend yield of the group (~3%). CEO: Michael Fiddelke.
How to think about the valuation gap

Walmart and Target both run higher gross margins than Costco, but the market pays up most for Costco. Why? Because Costco's membership fees produce reliable, high-quality earnings and its comparable-sales growth has been more consistent. Walmart's premium (vs. its own history) reflects e-commerce and advertising growth. Target trades at a discount because comp sales and traffic have been choppier.
