# registered-q10-en

Should I put my emergency fund in crypto?

**Generally, crypto does not fit the purpose of an emergency fund.** Emergency money needs to remain safe and accessible when an unexpected bill or loss of income occurs, while crypto can lose value quickly and may be difficult to withdraw precisely when the money is needed.

### Why the mismatch matters

An emergency fund is a cash reserve for unplanned expenses such as medical bills, repairs, or lost income. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says its location should be safe, accessible, and separate enough to discourage non-emergency spending.

Crypto introduces risks that conflict with those requirements:

- **Price risk:** Crypto investments can be exceptionally volatile and speculative, so the amount available during an emergency may be substantially less than the amount deposited.
- **Access risk:** A crypto platform can suspend withdrawals, fail, or enter bankruptcy. Customers may not recover all their assets or receive them when needed.
- **Custody risk:** Self-custody avoids some platform exposure but creates responsibility for private keys. Losing access credentials can mean losing access to the assets.
- **Protection gap:** The FDIC explicitly lists crypto assets among products it does not insure. Investor protections associated with regulated securities accounts also may not apply to accounts at crypto entities.
- **Conversion risk:** A bill denominated in USD generally requires selling or converting the crypto, adding an extra step between the reserve and the expense.

### Comparing the roles

| Consideration | Insured deposit account | Crypto holding |
|---|---|---|
| Principal stability | Deposit value does not fluctuate with crypto markets | Market value can change sharply |
| Federal deposit insurance | Eligible checking, savings, money-market deposit accounts, and certificates of deposit at an FDIC-insured bank are covered under applicable rules | Crypto assets are not FDIC-insured |
| Immediate usefulness for USD bills | Already held in USD | Usually must be sold or converted |
| Access risks | Subject to the account’s transfer and withdrawal terms | May also face platform failure, frozen withdrawals, wallet errors, or lost keys |
| Main role | Cash reserve and liquidity | Speculative investment exposure |

A stablecoin can reduce some market-price movement, but it does not automatically eliminate issuer, reserve, platform, custody, redemption, or insurance risk. Calling an asset “stable” is not the same as having an insured USD deposit.

### A useful way to separate the decision

Treat **emergency reserves** and **speculative investments** as different buckets. For the emergency bucket, compare options by principal stability, federal insurance eligibility, withdrawal speed, transfer limits, fees, and availability outside normal business hours. For any crypto bucket, evaluate it only as money exposed to potentially significant or total loss, not as guaranteed cash for the next emergency.
