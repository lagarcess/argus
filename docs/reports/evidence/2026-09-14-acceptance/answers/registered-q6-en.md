Is Apple expensive at this P/E?

Apple looks expensive on earnings alone: at 38.39x trailing earnings, investors are paying a substantial price for each dollar of current profit. That does not prove the shares are overvalued, but it means the valuation leaves less room for disappointing growth.

### What the valuation means

At the market price on 2026-09-14, Apple’s trailing earnings yield is 2.61%, the inverse of its P/E:

**P/E = share price ÷ trailing earnings per share**

**Earnings yield = trailing earnings per share ÷ share price**

This earnings yield is not a promised return. It simply expresses current trailing earnings relative to the share price.

### What supports the premium

Apple’s latest reported quarter, ended 2026-06-27, showed revenue growth of 16% and diluted EPS growth of 29% year over year. Those results support a higher valuation, but the EPS figure included a favorable tariff-refund impact of $0.11 per share, so not all of that quarter’s growth necessarily reflects recurring operations.

### The practical interpretation

| If future results show… | What the current P/E could imply |
|---|---|
| Strong, durable earnings growth | Today’s premium could become less demanding as earnings catch up |
| Slower growth | The shares could remain expensive even if earnings continue rising |
| A lower market valuation multiple | The price could fall despite positive business results |

The balanced conclusion is therefore **“expensive but supported by strong recent growth,” rather than automatically overvalued**. Whether the price is justified ultimately depends on future earnings growth and the valuation investors will assign to those earnings, neither is knowable from the current P/E alone.
