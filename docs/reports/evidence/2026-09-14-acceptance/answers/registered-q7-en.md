# registered-q7-en

Should I save in pesos or in dollars?

For savings intended to pay expenses in the United States, dollars avoid the exchange-rate risk created by converting pesos back to dollars. Peso savings are better viewed as money reserved for known peso-denominated expenses, not automatically as a higher-return substitute for dollar savings.

### How the options differ

| Option | Main benefit | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Dollars | Your savings and expected U.S. spending use the same currency | The account’s interest may not keep pace with inflation |
| Pesos | Matches future expenses priced in that particular peso and may offer a different interest rate | Exchange-rate movements can reduce the dollar value, while conversion fees and currency controls may limit access |
| Split between both | Matches separate spending needs in each currency | Adds conversion costs and account-management complexity |

### The key test

Compare each option in **dollar terms after interest, fees, taxes, and currency conversion**. A higher peso interest rate does not by itself mean a higher dollar return: changes in the exchange rate can increase or reduce the result.

Also verify what “pesos” means, since several countries use that name. The relevant inflation, deposit rate, conversion cost, banking protections, and exchange-rate risk depend on the specific country and account.

### Safety and access

For a U.S. bank account, eligible checking, savings, money-market deposit accounts, and certificates of deposit can receive FDIC protection when held at an insured bank and within the applicable coverage rules. A foreign account or currency product should be checked separately; do not assume it has the same protection or legal remedies. Some countries may also impose controls that restrict or delay moving currency out of the country.
