# registered-q8-en

Is my savings account actually losing money?

**Possibly.** Your dollar balance may be increasing while its purchasing power falls. As of 2026-08-31, U.S. consumer prices had risen 3.4% over the preceding year; if your account’s APY after fees is below that, it lost purchasing power over that period.

### How to check

Find the current **APY** on your statement or account page, not merely the interest credited last month. Savings rates are generally variable, while APY incorporates compounding.

| Comparison | Meaning |
|---|---|
| APY above inflation | Purchasing power increased before tax |
| APY equal to inflation | Purchasing power was broadly unchanged before tax |
| APY below inflation | Purchasing power decreased |

The latest national savings-account benchmark reported for August was only 0.38%. Its exact effective date could not be confirmed from the retrieved page, which was published on 2026-09-09. That benchmark is not necessarily your rate.

### The math

**Real return = (one plus APY) ÷ (one plus inflation) − one**

For an illustration using USD 1,000 at the national benchmark, with no deposits and both rates held constant for 1, the account ends at USD 1,003.80 nominally but has inflation-adjusted purchasing power of USD 970.79. Its real annual return is -2.92%.

This is a historical comparison, not a forecast: savings APYs and inflation can change. Account fees can reduce your effective return, and your own spending may rise faster or slower than the national consumer-price index.
