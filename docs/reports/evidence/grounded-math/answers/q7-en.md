# q7 (en)

**Asked:** Should I save in pesos or in dollars?

**Seconds to first answer:** 66.62. **Seconds to final answer:** 66.62.

There is no defensible single answer until “pesos” means a specific country and we know which currency you will spend. The central trade-off is currency matching: pesos avoid exchange-rate risk for peso expenses, while dollars avoid it for dollar expenses; holding both can match expenses in both currencies.

### How to frame the choice

| Situation | Peso savings | Dollar savings |
|---|---|---|
| Future expenses are mainly in pesos | Matches those expenses directly | Value in pesos changes with the exchange rate and conversion costs |
| Future expenses are mainly in dollars | Requires conversion later | Matches those expenses directly |
| Expenses will be in both currencies | Covers the peso portion | Covers the dollar portion |
| Goal or spending currency is uncertain | Concentrates exposure in pesos | Concentrates exposure in dollars |

Exchange rates move with factors including interest rates and economic and trade conditions, and even experienced market participants have difficulty predicting them. Currency conversion may also involve spreads, commissions and other costs. That means the higher advertised deposit rate is not automatically the better outcome after conversion.

### What needs to be compared

For actual peso and dollar accounts, compare:

- the deposit rate in each currency;
- expected fees and the bank’s conversion spread;
- whether the rate is fixed or variable;
- withdrawal restrictions and penalties;
- deposit-insurance protection in the country where the account is held;
- taxes on interest and currency gains or losses;
- the currency and timing of the future expense.

Current rates, inflation, exchange rates, taxes and deposit-insurance terms could not be identified because the peso country, account country and specific products were not provided. A live peso, dollar comparison should not be estimated without those details.

### Practical decision test

Start with the future expense rather than trying to predict which currency will rise. Money intended for peso expenses and money intended for dollar expenses face different risks, so they should be evaluated separately. For any amount whose spending currency is genuinely unknown, the relevant comparison is the cost of being wrong in either direction, not merely which account advertises more interest.
