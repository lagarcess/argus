# q7 (en)

**Asked:** Should I save in pesos or in dollars?

**Seconds to first answer:** 38.36. **Seconds to final answer:** 38.36.

Neither currency is universally better. The key distinction is **what currency you expect to spend the money in**: dollars remove exchange-rate uncertainty for dollar expenses, while pesos remove it for peso expenses; holding both can align separate savings goals with their eventual spending currencies.

### How the options differ

| Savings currency | Main benefit | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Dollars | The balance does not fluctuate in dollar terms | Its purchasing power for peso expenses changes with the exchange rate |
| Pesos | The balance does not fluctuate in peso terms and may earn a different deposit rate | Its dollar value can rise or fall with the exchange rate |
| Both currencies | Different goals can be matched to different spending currencies | More accounts, conversion costs and allocation decisions |

Foreign-currency holdings are exposed to exchange-rate movements, which can help or hurt their value when converted. The peso account’s advertised interest rate therefore does not provide a complete comparison with a dollar account: currency movement, conversion spreads and account fees also matter.

### A practical way to frame the decision

Separate the money by purpose:

- **Dollar obligations:** US living costs, tuition, taxes, debt payments or a dollar emergency reserve.
- **Peso obligations:** living costs, property expenses, family support or planned purchases in the country issuing the pesos.
- **Uncertain future spending:** compare how much exposure to each currency corresponds to the plausible spending destinations, rather than relying only on which account currently advertises the higher rate.

### Information needed for a precise comparison

“Pesos” could mean Mexican, Dominican, Philippine or another country’s currency. A proper comparison also requires the exact accounts, their deposit rates, fees, conversion spreads, tax treatment, withdrawal restrictions and deposit-insurance arrangements. No live rate or exchange-rate figure is used here because the peso currency and products were not specified.
