# q10 (en)

**Asked:** Should I put my emergency fund in crypto?

**Seconds to first answer:** 34.52. **Seconds to final answer:** 34.52.

It's understandable why you're asking, crypto can feel exciting, and the idea of your emergency savings growing faster sounds appealing. But here's the honest context: an emergency fund is meant to be safe and immediately accessible when life throws something unexpected your way, like a job loss, car repair, or medical bill.

Cryptocurrencies are known for their volatility, prices can drop 30-50% in a short period. If you needed your emergency money during a downturn, you might have to sell at a loss, which defeats the purpose of having that safety net. Most people keep emergency funds in stable, liquid accounts like high-yield savings or money market accounts, where the balance doesn't swing wildly.

If you're curious about crypto for other savings goals, say, long-term investing or learning more about the space, that's a different conversation entirely. A nearby experiment: you could try setting aside a very small amount (that you'd be comfortable losing entirely) in a stablecoin or low-risk crypto product, just to see how it feels to manage and access it, without touching your actual emergency savings.
