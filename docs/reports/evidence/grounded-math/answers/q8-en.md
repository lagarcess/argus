# q8 (en)

**Asked:** Is my savings account actually losing money?

**Seconds to first answer:** 47.58. **Seconds to final answer:** 47.58.

**Possibly.** Your balance may be increasing in dollars while losing purchasing power if your savings account’s after-tax yield is below inflation. As of 2026-08-31, US consumer prices were 3.4% higher than a year earlier; your account’s current APY and your tax situation are needed for a definite answer.

### The test

| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nominal return | Interest credited, minus account fees |
| After-tax return | Nominal return after taxes on the interest |
| Real return | After-tax return adjusted for inflation |

A precise comparison is:

**Real return = (1 + after-tax account return) ÷ (1 + inflation rate) − 1**

- A negative result means the money lost purchasing power.
- A positive result means purchasing power increased.
- An unchanged account balance still loses purchasing power whenever prices rise.

### Why taxes matter

Interest on ordinary US bank accounts is generally taxable in the year it becomes available. Therefore, merely matching inflation with the advertised APY may not preserve purchasing power after federal, and potentially state, income tax. Fees can reduce the return further.

### What to check

Look at the account’s current APY, interest earned, and fees on your latest statement. Compare the return after fees and taxes with 3.4%, while recognizing that national inflation may differ from the price changes affecting your own spending. The inflation figure describes the past year, not what prices will do next.
