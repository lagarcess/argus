Microsoft's Revenue Streams and Businesses
How does Microsoft make money and what are its main businesses?

Microsoft makes most of its money by selling cloud-computing capacity and AI services, subscriptions to workplace and business software, and software licenses. It also earns revenue from gaming, advertising, devices, professional-network services, and technical support. Microsoft reports these activities in three main business segments. [1]

For its fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, the latest full year available as of September 3, 2026, Microsoft generated $331.839 billion in revenue and $133.749 billion in net income. [1]

1. Productivity and Business Processes

This is Microsoft’s largest segment by FY2026 revenue. Its core products are Microsoft 365, LinkedIn and Dynamics. [1]

Microsoft 365: Businesses and consumers pay recurring subscriptions for applications and services such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams, OneDrive, security products and Microsoft 365 Copilot. Microsoft also still sells some on-premises Office and Windows Commercial licenses. [1]
LinkedIn: Revenue comes from recruiting and talent products, sales tools, premium memberships and advertising through Marketing Solutions. [1]
Dynamics: Companies pay for cloud and on-premises enterprise-resource-planning and customer-relationship-management software, including Dynamics 365, Power Apps and Power Automate. Revenue is influenced by licensed users, applications consumed and revenue per user. [1]
2. Intelligent Cloud

This segment contains Azure and Microsoft’s server, developer and enterprise-service businesses. [1]

Azure: Customers generally pay according to how much cloud infrastructure and platform capacity they consume, such as computing, networking, storage, databases, AI and application services. [1]
Server and developer products: Microsoft licenses SQL Server, Windows Server, Visual Studio, System Center and related access rights. GitHub provides both cloud and on-premises developer tools, including AI-assisted offerings. [1]
Enterprise services: Microsoft charges for support, consulting, implementation assistance, training and certification. [1]

This was Microsoft’s fastest-growing major segment in FY2026: revenue rose 30%, with Azure and other cloud-services revenue increasing 41%. Microsoft reported $214.4 billion of Microsoft Cloud revenue, a broader measure spanning several segments. [1]

3. More Personal Computing

This segment combines Microsoft’s consumer-facing and PC-related businesses. [1]

Windows: PC manufacturers pay to license and preinstall Windows on computers; organizations also purchase commercial Windows licenses. [1]
Xbox: Revenue comes from game and in-game-content sales, Xbox Game Pass and other subscriptions, advertising, digital stores, cloud gaming and console hardware. [1]
Search advertising: Advertisers pay Microsoft to place search, native and display ads across services including Bing, Copilot, Edge and affiliated properties. [1]
Devices: Microsoft sells Surface computers and PC accessories. [1]
The simple picture

Microsoft now has a mixed model:

Subscriptions for Microsoft 365, Copilot, Dynamics, LinkedIn and Xbox services.
Usage-based charges for Azure cloud and AI workloads.
Software licensing for Windows, Office and server products.
Advertising through search, LinkedIn and gaming.
Transaction and content sales through games, digital stores and related services.
Hardware sales and professional services from Surface, Xbox consoles, accessories, consulting and support. [1]

The economic center of the company is therefore enterprise cloud infrastructure plus recurring business-software subscriptions, although Windows, gaming and advertising remain sizable businesses. In FY2026, Productivity and Business Processes and Intelligent Cloud together generated $277.787 billion, or 83.7% of Microsoft’s total revenue. [1]
