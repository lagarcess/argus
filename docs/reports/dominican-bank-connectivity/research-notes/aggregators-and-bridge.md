# U4. Aggregator coverage of Dominican financial institutions

Research date 2026-09-27. Public pages only. No account, demo request, form, connection, or contact was made with any company. Every source below was seen on 2026-09-27; publication or last-updated dates are given where the page shows one.

Evidence labels (one per claim):

- Verified through documentation. The provider's public legal or developer documentation states it, or an independent source (a bank, a regulator, an inspectable institution list) confirms it. For an institution list, this label means the provider documents the connector. It does not mean the connector works.
- Observed on public pages. I saw it myself on a public page, video, directory, or listing.
- Vendor claim only. Marketing or self-description with no independent confirmation.
- Unknown. No public evidence either way.
- Requires an authorized account test. Only a signed-up developer account, a live connection, or a provider conversation can settle it.

## Bottom line

- No aggregator publicly names a Dominican institution today. Bridge claims Dominican coverage but names no bank on any public page. It reads accounts by logging in with the user's online-banking credentials, and its bank list is visible only inside Bridge Connect after developer signup.
- Salt Edge scraped Banco Popular Dominicano, Banreservas, and Banco BHD León (and Banco Dominicano del Progreso in beta) between 2017 and 2021, then dropped every Dominican connector by July 2022. Its live Dominican page shows 0 connections.
- Prometeo has named the Dominican Republic as a planned market since 2024, most recently in January 2026, and documents no Dominican bank.
- No Dominican fintech other than Bridge was found that connects bank accounts for a third party to read. InSync claims to but has not launched. tPago reads accounts only as a bank-enrolled payment service.
- No Superintendencia de Bancos or Banco Central page names Bridge or any data aggregator. The SB lists open banking as a project tagged S2 2026, and no draft or consultation is published.

## 2. Bridge deep-dive (bridge.com.do)

Bridge is the only provider found that is built for the Dominican Republic. It publishes no list of supported institutions anywhere public. Its own terms describe credential-based access, where Bridge logs in to the user's online banking on the user's behalf, and they disclaim any affiliation with the banks.

Name collisions to avoid. "Bridge API" at bridgeapi.io is a French aggregator, "Bridge" at bridge.xyz is Stripe's stablecoin company, "Third Bridge" is an expert-research firm, and "BRIDGE Labs" on the App Store is a Korean app developer. None of these is the Dominican Bridge.

### 2.13 Inconsistencies to raise with Bridge

- The privacy policy says credentials are not stored (B30), while the Connect terms keep encrypted session tokens for silent re-login (B31), and the consent screen asks for "future transactions" (B25). Ongoing refresh therefore depends on stored bank session tokens. Ask how long they last per bank and how often users must reconnect.
- The homepage and docs say Bridge cannot read the data without the app's scoped token (B33, B36). The privacy policy lists OpenAI for AI-based personalization (B38), and the API has a `/categorize` endpoint (B6). Ask whether transaction text leaves Bridge in plaintext, to whom, and when it is decrypted.
- Bright Data proxies in the UK, US, and Israel (B23) mean some external connections leave the Dominican Republic through a third party. Ask whether bank sessions, and so credentials in transit, go through those proxies.

## Limits of this report

- The provider rows other than Bridge, Section 3, and rows B55 to B55e rest on the three sweep agents' full reports, which list every page they read. I did not re-open those pages, because the coordinator directed no new web requests at finalization.
- The Prometeo evidence relies on Wayback Machine copies of docs pages that were public when captured and allowed by robots.txt, but that Prometeo has since put behind a password. The coordinator may prefer to discount them.

No CAPTCHA was solved.
- Process slips. I saved Bridge's homepage HTML to the scratchpad once and deleted it at once. The Latin American sweep wrote two scratch files under /tmp and deleted them. Two sweep requests went out in the same command as a robots.txt check that disallowed them: a Finerio docs sitemap index (an index of four URLs, no page content) and TrueLayer's auth-host provider list (not used in any claim).
