# Scotiabank RD, APAP, and other Dominican institutions: public evidence for user-authorized account data

Research lane u3b. Every row was seen on 2026-09-27. Public pages only.

## How to read this file

- Every row carries one evidence label: Verified through documentation, Observed on public pages, Vendor claim only, Unknown, or Requires an authorized account test.
- "Verified through documentation" means an official help page, user manual, FAQ, terms, tariff, contract template, or regulator publication describes the fact concretely. For the status and size rows, that documentation is a Superintendencia de Bancos (SB) page or the institution's own annual report.
- Marketing and product pages are "Observed on public pages". Their authenticated behavior stays unverified.
- Statements made by an aggregator, a directory, a wallet operator, or the press about an institution are "Vendor claim only".
- "Unknown" rows record a documented absence, a blocked page, or a fact no public page states.
- "Requires an authorized account test" rows mark a gap that only a logged-in customer can close. The last section lists those tests.
- Nothing here came from signing in, enrolling, submitting a form, installing an app, or contacting an institution.
- Limitation. The session's shared web-search quota (200 searches) ran out early in this lane. Later checks used direct page fetches, sitemaps, and on-site links. News coverage of 2024 to 2026 corporate events therefore rests on the few searches made before that point, plus the regulator and company pages cited below.

## Decision summary

- **Status.** Scotiabank RD is "Scotiabank República Dominicana, S. A. - Banco Múltiple", operating, a subsidiary of The Bank of Nova Scotia. No 2024 to 2026 sale, merger, or conversion was found. APAP is still "Asociación Popular de Ahorros y Préstamos", a mutualist savings and loan association under Law 5897 of 1962. It has not converted into a bank. The SB list of Junta Monetaria regulations, newest entry 16 July 2026, has no conversion regulation.
- **Size.** By SB total assets, APAP ranks 5th with 4.2% and Scotiabank 6th with 3.9% of 47 intermediaries. Banco Santa Cruz ranks 4th with 5.0%. It is larger than both and is not one of the five candidates.
- **APIs.** Neither institution publishes a consumer account-data API, a developer program, or an aggregator partnership. Scotiabank's group developer portal offers business payment and account-information APIs, lists open banking as coming soon, and never mentions the Dominican Republic.
- **Best documented self-service data.** Scotiabank offers free statements in Scotia en Línea and its app, with no documented file format. It also sends free email or push alerts for purchases, ATM withdrawals, transfers, bill payments, and daily or weekly balances. APAP's FAQ documents 12 months of filterable history on the web and an in-app HolAPAP notice for every card transaction. Neither documents a personal CSV, Excel, or OFX export. APAP's business portal exports history to PDF or Excel.
- **Currencies.** Scotiabank offers savings accounts in DOP, USD, and EUR. APAP lists no USD or EUR deposit account. Its dollar amounts are the US$ balance on international credit cards and a branch currency-exchange service.
- **Credential sharing.** Scotiabank's online-banking terms make the customer liable for access by anyone given the username or password. Its online-security guarantee requires the customer to have met security duties. APAP tells customers never to share credentials and publishes no copy of its personal online-banking terms. Neither has a readable clause on scraping or automated access.
- **Friction for credential-based aggregation.** Scotiabank requires multi-factor enrollment and emails an access code. Its security FAQ keeps at most three registered computers, so a fourth drops the first. Its general FAQ says idle sessions close after 10 minutes. APAP links each device with a one-time code and ties transfer approval to a single device through Mi Llave. Whether viewing balances needs a fresh code is unknown for both.
- **Aggregators.** Bridge names no institution on its public pages, so its coverage of Scotiabank and APAP is Unknown. Bridge Connect collects the user's bank credentials. tPago already shows balances and history for both, and both institutions document it, but tPago offers no third-party access. Salt Edge lists 0 Dominican connections.
- **Part 2.** No other Dominican institution offers a materially better entry point. SB lists open-banking regulation as an in-progress project for the second half of 2026 and has published no draft.

## Notes

- **Method.** Three research agents covered Scotiabank dimensions 2 to 6, APAP dimensions 2 to 6, and the Part 2 scan. This lane's coordinator covered legal status, size, aggregators, and the regulator items, and re-checked the most decision-relevant agent claims against raw page text. The re-checked claims include the Scotiabank credential and legal-notice clauses, alerts, 10-minute timeout, MFA FAQ, tariffs, and online-banking hosts, and the APAP 12-month history, business export, Mi Llave, tariff, legal notice, and online-banking hosts.
- **Size data.** Figures are what each SB entity page showed on 2026-09-27. The update stamps range from 6 Aug to 15 Sep 2026.
- **robots.txt.** Every fetched domain's robots.txt was checked. For sb.gob.do, acento.com.do, and diariolibre.com the check came after the first page fetch, and every fetched path is allowed. noticiassin.com serves a Cloudflare challenge even for robots.txt, and its article was not read. do.scotiabank.com disallows the subfolders of its Dominican asset library, so the Convenio v9, the credit-card contract, the app guide, and loan summary sheets were not fetched. A person can still open them in a browser. apap.com.do, bridge.com.do, tpago.com, and sb.gob.do allow the paths used. The online-banking hosts of both institutions publish no robots.txt. Open Banking Tracker's robots.txt allows all paths. Its pages returned a Vercel security checkpoint to command-line fetches and were read in a normal browser tab without solving any challenge.
- **Blocks.** Noticias SIN and Banco Caribe returned HTTP 403. www.apap.com.do stopped answering one agent for 12 to 15 minutes after about 65 sequential requests. The agent waited and resumed at 6 to 8 seconds per request. ProUsuario's contract list needs a form filter and was not used.
- **Process deviations.** The APAP agent's mistaken curl option wrote a 2,772-byte file under /tmp. The agent deleted it within a minute. The fetch tool also cached a few PDFs on its own under this session's tool-results folder, which is outside any git repository. No file was written inside a git repository.
- **Inference flagged.** The row saying Scotiabank's security guarantee likely excludes losses after credential sharing is an inference from two documents, not a stated term.
- **Legacy wording.** Scotiabank's security FAQ mentions 36.6K modems and the older Scotia Móvil app. Its three-computer limit and 10-minute timeout may predate the current Scotia Caribbean platform.
- **Bridge and Argus.** Bridge's public plans cap the free developer plan at 3 active personal connections and the standard plan at 50. Its API exposes enough fields for balances and transactions. No public page lists its supported banks, so seeing the list appears to need a developer account and the Connect widget.
