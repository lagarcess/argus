# Dominican open finance and financial-sector rules for read-only account aggregation

Every source was read and accessed on 2026-09-27.

This is legal research, not a legal opinion. It does not say that any activity is legal or illegal. It records what official texts say, what they do not say, and what Dominican counsel or the regulator should answer.

Method. Public sources only, read with WebFetch, and BCRD pages rendered in the desktop browser pane because they load by script. PDFs were read from their text layer (pdftotext) or page images, not from automated summaries. One automated summary of SB Circular 004/23 gave wrong dates and limits, so every provision below was checked against the document text. No sign-in, account, form submission or contact with any institution. The session's web-search allowance (200 searches, shared with other agents) ran out partway through. Later discovery used the official listing pages of the SB and the BCRD.

Labels. "Primary" means an official publication of the issuing authority. "Secondary" means press or third-party text, used only to locate a primary source or where no primary source exists. "Inference" marks the researcher's reading, not a statement of any authority. "Unknown" and "Not found" mean exactly that.

Abbreviations. JM is the Junta Monetaria. BCRD is the Banco Central de la República Dominicana. SB is the Superintendencia de Bancos. EIF is an entidad de intermediación financiera (a bank or other licensed lender and deposit-taker). PSP is a proveedor de servicios de pago. EPE is an entidad de pago electrónico. SIC is a sociedad de información crediticia (credit bureau). CNLA is the Comité Nacional contra el Lavado de Activos. Record numbers S1 to S24 point to the appendix.

## 2. Narrative notes by topic

### Topic 1. Open finance

No law, JM reglamento, SB instructivo, circular, API standard, participant list or binding timeline on open finance or open banking was found as of 2026-09-27. The BCRD lists of reglamentos in force and in process, the BCRD instructivos list, the SB reglamentos list, the recent SB normativas, and the SB public-consultations page contain nothing on it (M25).

What exists is a sequence of announcements, strategies and events:

- 2022-02-11. The BCRD and the superintendencies launch the Hub de Innovación Financiera (M7).
- 2022-06-09. The SB signs a commitment letter with the IFC and the World Bank for an open banking pilot and an API framework (M5).
- 2022-08-10. The ENIF 2022-2030 plans the study and development of open finance schemes for 2022 to 2030, led by the JM, BCRD and SB, and sets up an open finance working group (M6).
- 2024-12-09. At a Hub and Adofintech workshop, the Superintendent says regulators are working on a normative framework (M9).
- 2025-01-21. Secondary. IUPANA reports that fintechs expected a public consultation in 2025 (S21). No consultation was found in primary sources.
- 2025-09-23. The III Open Banking Forum names no rule or date (M10).
- Undated page read 2026-09-27. The SB innovation page lists an Open Banking project labelled "S2 2026", ending in the drafting of a normativa (M11). No draft has been published.
- 2026-09-01. Enmanuel Cedeño Brea replaces Alejandro Fernández W. as Superintendent. His 2026-09-04 priorities do not mention open banking (S20).

Data scope. No official scope exists. The SB describes consent-based exchange of customer data among EIFs and other providers. The ENIF defines open finance as the exchange of financial information among authorized entities, third-party providers and merchants, with open banking as the usual first stage. Payment initiation is regulated on its own by the 2025 payments reglamento (M12). Account information is not defined in any text found.

Third parties. No accreditation or registration regime for data recipients exists. The only third-party role defined is the payment initiation provider, registered with the BCRD (M12).

Inference. Under Ley 183-02 art. 4(f)-(g), duties on third parties would most likely come through a JM reglamento, which needs a public consultation of at least 30 days before adoption (M3). A published draft would therefore give at least a month's notice. The instrument and issuer are Unknown.

### Topic 2. Financial confidentiality

Art. 56(b) of Ley 183-02 was rewritten by art. 362 of Ley 249-17 (2017-12-19) as an obligación de confidencialidad (M1). It covers EIFs and securities-market participants, and its last sentence extends it to other financial-market entities that carry out or record operations with the public. It protects deposits, investments and other financial operations that reveal client identity or transaction details.

Exceptions. Personalized data may go to the account holder or to "a la persona que éste autorice expresamente", by any reliable means admitted in law. The tax administration, the financial intelligence unit, the BCRD, the SB and the securities regulator may request data directly within their competences. Courts may order disclosure, and prosecutors may request it with judicial authorization. Breach is punished under the Penal Code rules on professional secrecy (art. 56(b)) and is a very serious infraction for EIFs (art. 68(a)(13)).

Sharing at the customer's request. Inference: the holder-authorization exception is the legal basis for a bank to release data to a third party the customer chooses. The text does not say what form the authorization takes, whether it can be standing or given inside a third party's app, how long it lasts, or whether the bank must verify it. The duty falls on the bank, not on the recipient. The recipient's duties would come from contract, from Ley 172-13 (not read) and from any future rule.

Interpretations found. Art. 38 of the 2015 user-protection reglamento lets the SB information service answer requests from persons entitled under art. 56(b) (M15). No SB circular, JM resolution or court decision interpreting customer-directed sharing with a non-supervised third party was found. A bank-association article reviewed as a pointer cites none (S24, secondary).

Credential-based access. Inference: when a user gives an aggregator online-banking credentials, the bank does not disclose anything by its own act. Art. 56(b) may then not be the governing rule. Bank contracts, the cybersecurity reglamento and the pending user-protection reform become the relevant texts (Topics 3 and 4).

### Topic 3. Financial consumer rights

Current version. The reglamento in force is the JM Primera Resolución of 2015-02-05, with arts. 4, 8 and 38 changed by the JM Cuarta Resolución of 2015-09-30 (published 2016-02-11). The BCRD still lists it as current on 2026-09-27 (M15). A comprehensive reform was put to consultation by the JM Cuarta Resolución of 2024-09-05, published 2025-03-03, and remains in process (M16).

Information and statements. Art. 6 gives users the right to exact and complete information on products, to all documents on the product and later changes, to the information the entity generates about them with correction of errors, and to what the entity reports about them to the SB risk registry and credit bureaus. Art. 7(d) requires entities to keep information on the state of the user's accounts and products, with the operations made, available to the user. Art. 19(c) requires payment receipts on request, in paper or electronic form. No text requires a machine-readable format or delivery to a third party.

Electronic channels. Art. 25 allows contracting through electronic platforms and lets the SB issue an instructivo. Electronic channels must confirm transaction results and let users consult the history of these operations.

Data use. Art. 7(c) requires entities to protect user information for confidentiality, availability and integrity. The draft reform would add consent for any transfer of data (art. 5(h)), a right to know recipients and purpose (art. 6(i)), and privacy policies covering access by contracted third parties (art. 11).

User duties on credentials. The current text has no user-duty article and no credential rule (full-text search for clave, contraseña and deberes). The draft adds a duty to follow the security measures the entity indicates, to protect personal and financial data (art. 9(f)). It places fraud losses on the user only for the user's fraud, intentional breach or gross negligence (art. 136). It clears the user when losses come from weak authentication controls attributable to the entity (art. 137(b)). Inference: if adopted, these rules and bank contracts will decide who bears losses when a user has shared credentials with an aggregator.

### Topic 4. Cybersecurity

The Reglamento de Seguridad Cibernética y de la Información was approved by the JM Segunda Resolución of 2018-11-01 and published 2018-11-27. Its header records no modification, and none was found (M17). The BCRD lists an Instructivo para el Cumplimiento del Reglamento as current, but it could not be read (M18).

Customer authentication and credentials. Art. 27(b) requires identity management covering identification, authentication and access control. Art. 23 requires entities to inventory and register customer access to electronic or digital channels and to protect it with control and supervision mechanisms. No explicit multi-factor rule for customers was found in the reglamento text. Any such rule would sit in the unread instructivo (Unknown).

Third parties. Art. 14 requires banks to assess the technology risk of interconnected entities, disconnect those that fail, and refuse new interconnections whose assessment is not satisfactory. Art. 5(aa) defines an entidad interconectada as a legal person that keeps an electronic connection or information exchange with an EIF under a contract. Art. 3 extends the reglamento to support and related-services entities linked electronically where the link can affect the payment system. Art. 5(y) includes software providers. Art. 36 requires security clauses in contracts with technology providers.

Cloud. Art. 36(d) requires a documented policy for using and contracting cloud services, including web hosting, backed by a risk analysis. No data-location rule was found in this reglamento.

Incident reporting. Art. 31 requires incident-management policies (identify, respond, remediate, document, forensics). Arts. 49 to 58 create a sector council and a financial-sector CSIRT under the BCRD, to which the security officer reports periodically (art. 11). No reporting deadline to the SB was found in the reglamento. The 2026 draft operational-risk instructivo would require immediate notice of material events, including cyberattacks and client-data disclosure, and a report within 10 days (M21).

Inference for Argus. Screen-scraping access happens through the channels banks must inventory and protect. A bank API partnership would make Argus an interconnected entity subject to the bank's risk assessment and possible disconnection.

### Topic 5. Outsourcing

Three texts apply to supervised entities.

1. The SB Instructivo sobre Tercerización, Circular SB No. 011/12 of 2012-12-28, is still in force (M20).
2. The new Reglamento sobre Riesgo Operacional, JM Cuarta Resolución of 2025-11-27, published 2026-04-21, sets outsourcing management duties (M19).
3. Art. 36 of the cybersecurity reglamento covers technology providers and cloud services (M17).

- Due diligence. The SB evaluates the board-approved policy, the feasibility analysis, due diligence on the provider, the written contract, contingency plans and confidentiality (011/12 section 4; RO arts. 51 to 53).
- SB notification or authorization. Material outsourcing needs prior SB no-objection. The filing includes the board resolution, the risk assessment and the contract form (011/12 section 3). Listed low-risk services need no prior authorization (section 5).
- Cross-border processing and data location. No absolute localization rule was found. Before keeping or processing data outside the Dominican Republic, the bank must describe the data to the SB. The contract must give the SB access to all data, including at backup and recovery subcontractors (011/12 section 6). For foreign providers, the bank must confirm no law blocks SB access (section 2.4).
- Cloud hosting. Covered by the cloud policy in cybersecurity art. 36(d). The 2012 instructivo does not mention cloud services (Inference from full-text search).
- Audit rights. Contracts must give the entity audit rights (011/12 section III item 7). They must also guarantee audit trails and independent control reports (RO art. 52) and let the SB review outsourced data processing at the provider (RO art. 89 párrafo II).

Relevance to Argus. Inference: if a bank offers Argus features to its customers as its own service, the arrangement may count as tercerización de servicios (RO art. 4(yy)), with no-objection and cross-border steps. If the bank only sends data at the customer's direction, the outsourcing texts may not apply. Neither case is addressed in any text found.

### Topic 6. Payments versus information

The Reglamento de Sistemas de Pago was comprehensively revised by the JM Segunda Resolución of 2025-08-28, published 2025-10-21, replacing the 2021 text (M12 to M14). The BCRD also has in-force instructivos for EPEs and electronic payment accounts and for payment-system administrators and other PSPs.

What is defined and regulated:

- Payment service. A financial service by a PSP that allows transfers of funds with a payment instrument (art. 4(jjj)).
- Payment initiation. Starting payment orders on the user's express instruction and verifiable consent, sent through secure interfaces, without the provider receiving, holding or managing the funds (art. 4(mm)). BCRD registration and duties apply (arts. 38 to 40).
- EPE. An exclusive-purpose company authorized by the JM to provide payment services through electronic payment accounts (arts. 4(ee), 15). JM authorization and capital apply.
- Digital wallet. An app that lets users initiate and/or receive transfers and payments through linked accounts or instruments (arts. 4(i), 36). BCRD registration applies.
- Agregador de pago. This merchant payment-facilitation role was renamed empresa de subadquirencia. It is not a data aggregator.

Account information. Not defined or regulated in any text found, including a full-text search of the 2025 payments reglamento.

Financial intermediation. Ley 183-02 art. 3(b) defines it as habitually taking funds from the public to lend them to third parties. The JM refused to let EPEs lend client funds because that would make them intermediaries (S18).

How read-only aggregation differs (Inference). It takes no funds, so it is not intermediation. It executes no transfers, so it is not a payment service. It sends no payment orders, so it is not payment initiation. It has no transfer function, so it is not a digital wallet. It holds no stored value, so it is not an EPE. Adding a pay or transfer button would move the service into the payment initiation or wallet categories, which need BCRD registration and bring AML duties (art. 43).

Practical note (Inference). In Dominican payments rules, agregador names a merchant payment role, so Spanish-language filings should describe Argus in other words.

### Topic 7. Innovation

Hub de Innovación Financiera. Launched 2022-02-11 by the BCRD, SB, SIMV, Superseguros and SIPEN (M7). It is open to regulated and unregulated innovators, offers free consultation and meetings, and assesses projects outside current law case by case, possibly referring them for legal reform. It grants no licence, exemption or test environment. An aggregation project could use it for consultation.

SB test environment. Circular SB 004/23 of 2023-05-25 (M8) lets only EIFs apply for SB no-objection. Tests may include more than one supervised entity, each filing its own request. The ambiente extraordinario mode fits activities not covered by current rules and not under another regulator. Limits are 5% of users and 6 months, extendable. Inference: an aggregation pilot could enter only as part of a bank's application. No text gives the non-supervised partner any status.

Payments test environment. Art. 83 of the 2025 payments reglamento lets authorized PSPs seek BCRD no-objection for tests involving other PSPs or external users (M14). The procedural instructivo was not listed on 2026-09-27. Inference: it does not fit a read-only service that is not a PSP.

Sandbox. The ENIF plans the feasibility and design of a regulatory sandbox for 2026 to 2030 (M6). No sandbox open to non-supervised firms was found.

### Topic 8. Licensing

No licence, registration or accreditation specific to read-only aggregation of a customer's own bank data was found. The rules are silent on the activity.

Adjacent regimes that a design choice could trigger (Inference):

- Taking or holding funds. Financial intermediation needs prior authorization (M2). An EPE needs JM authorization (M13).
- Initiating payments. BCRD registration as a payment initiation provider (M12).
- Offering transfers or payments through linked accounts. BCRD registration as a digital wallet provider (M13).
- Collecting consumer financial data and supplying or transmitting it to others, such as lenders. Possible SIC status, which needs JM authorization and SB registration under a broad definition (M22).
- Receiving bank equity investment. Possible support-entity registration with the SB (M4).
- Contracting with banks for data access. No licence, but bank-side outsourcing and cybersecurity duties flow down by contract (M17, M19, M20).
- Processing personal data. Ley 172-13 duties, not read (M24).

The question for counsel. Is any licence, registration or accreditation required today for a company that, on the customer's express authorization, retrieves and displays only that customer's own balances and transactions, never holds or moves funds, never initiates payments, and never supplies the data to third parties? Would the answer change if access is by the customer's online-banking credentials rather than by a bank API?

### Topic 9. AML

Ley 155-17 (2017-06-01) lists the financial obliged subjects in art. 32. They are EIFs, securities intermediaries, currency exchange and remittance intermediaries, the BCRD, licensed fiduciaries, savings and credit cooperatives, insurers and brokers, fund administrators, securitizers, stock brokers, the central securities depository, and certain public-offering issuers. Art. 33 lists the non-financial obliged subjects: casinos, factoring firms, real-estate agents, dealers in precious metals and vehicles, pawnshops, builders, and lawyers, notaries and accountants for listed client transactions. Art. 2(24) defines a sujeto obligado as a person the law obliges to prevent, detect, assess and mitigate money-laundering and terrorist-financing risk (OCR text, wording to be confirmed).

Inference: a read-only aggregator is not in any listed category. Two caveats apply. First, the CNLA may add activities by regulation (art. 32 párrafo I, art. 33 párrafo); no such addition was verified. Second, if Argus became a PSP, the payments reglamento would apply Ley 155-17 to it (art. 43) and require AML controls (art. 39(k)). The lawyers and accountants category covers professionals managing client bank accounts, not information services (Inference).
