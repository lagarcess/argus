# Legal and regulatory evidence matrix

This file is legal research for the Dominican bank-connectivity lane. It is not a legal opinion, and it does not say that any practice is legal or illegal. It records what official texts say, what they leave open, and the question a Dominican lawyer or regulator must answer. Every source was read on 2026-09-27. Primary sources are official publications. Secondary sources, marked as such, only located a primary source or filled a gap where no primary copy could be opened.

The research notes behind this file read the official texts through their text layers. The report author re-read four provisions directly in the official PDFs: Penal Code articles 188, 198, 199, and 393, and article 39 of the 2025 payments reglamento.

## The short version

- **No open-finance rule exists.** On 2026-09-27 no Dominican law, Junta Monetaria reglamento, Superintendencia de Bancos instructivo or circular, API standard, participant list, or dated timeline on open finance was in force or in public consultation. What exists is a 2022 pilot commitment, a 2022 national strategy, workshop and forum speeches, and a Superintendencia de Bancos project page labelled "S2 2026" with no published draft. A new Superintendent took office on 2026-09-01.
- **No licence covers read-only aggregation.** The rules neither license nor forbid a service that shows a customer only that customer's own balances and transactions. Design choices can trigger other regimes: payment initiation, digital wallets, electronic payment entities, and possibly credit-information companies.
- **A bank may share at the customer's express authorization.** Ley 183-02 article 56(b) lets a bank release a customer's data to a person the customer expressly authorizes. No text says what form that authorization takes or who verifies it.
- **Credential sharing meets four separate headwinds.** A bank contract approved in 2026 makes access codes personal and forbids sharing digital-channel credentials. The regulator's consumer office tells people never to share passwords. The 2025 payments reglamento forbids payment-initiation providers from storing user credentials. Criminal provisions on unauthorized access and identity use exist, and nobody has settled how they apply to consented access.
- **Data protection applies, with open questions.** Ley 172-13 sets consent, transfer, rights, and security duties. Its reach beyond credit bureaus, a credit-history reservation in article 30, and the treatment of transfers abroad need counsel. It sets no breach-notice duty and no localization rule. Two new data-protection bills filed in 2026 have not passed a chamber.

## Keep five questions apart

The assignment asks not to merge these. Dominican law answers each one differently.

1. **The person authorizing access.** Ley 172-13 requires free, express, informed consent after notice of purpose, recipients, and rights, with a contract-necessity exception. Ley 126-02 gives data messages the effect of writing. Whether an in-app consent screen satisfies the consent form is a question for counsel.
2. **A statutory right of access or portability.** A customer has a right to information the bank generates about them and to the state of their accounts under the 2015 user-protection reglamento, and a constitutional hábeas data action. No text gives a right to a machine-readable export or to delivery to a third party. No open-finance right exists.
3. **The bank's contractual conditions.** The Banco BHD personal-account contract model approved by the Superintendencia de Bancos on 2026-06-24 makes the access code personal and non-transferable, makes the customer responsible for its custody, and commits the customer not to share digital-channel credentials with third parties, according to the research note that read it. A pending reform of the user-protection reglamento would put fraud losses on a customer who acted with gross negligence.
4. **Authorization to operate a service.** No licence, registration, or accreditation exists for read-only aggregation. Payment initiation needs Banco Central registration. A digital wallet needs registration. An electronic payment entity needs Junta Monetaria authorization. A credit-information company needs Junta Monetaria authorization and registration under a broad 2026 definition.
5. **Duties when handling credentials and financial data.** Ley 172-13 imposes security and secrecy duties with criminal penalties behind them. Ley 53-07 and the new Penal Code punish unauthorized access, misuse of access codes, identity use, and automated collection of data without consent. The only Dominican third-party access role, payment initiation, may not store credentials.

## Matrix

Dominican financial rules come in three tiers under Ley 183-02 article 4. The Junta Monetaria issues reglamentos, the Banco Central and the Superintendencia de Bancos issue instructivos, and their individual acts are circulares.

Status uses five values: enacted and effective, enacted not yet effective, draft in consultation, bill, and announced. "Inference" marks a reading by the research, not a statement of any authority.

## Questions for Dominican counsel, in priority order

1. Is any licence, registration, or accreditation required for a company that, on the customer's express authorization, retrieves and displays only that customer's own balances and transactions, never holds or moves funds, never initiates payments, and never supplies the data to third parties? Does the answer change when access uses the customer's online-banking credentials instead of a bank API or a file the customer downloads?
2. For credential-based access with consent, how do Ley 53-07 articles 4 to 6, Penal Code articles 188 and 198 as amended by Ley 44-26, and bank contracts like BHD's 2026 model apply to Argus, to a provider such as Bridge, and to the customer?
3. For software running in the customer's own authenticated session, is that the customer's own access, and does automated reading exceed an authorization when the bank's terms forbid automation?
4. Does Ley 172-13 bind Argus as a foreign-hosted controller, and is consent or contract necessity the basis for storing Dominican users' data in the United States? What must the consent say?
5. Could Ley 172-13 article 30 or the 2026 credit-information instructivo reach an app that holds a user's own loan and card history?
6. What form must a customer's express authorization take under Ley 183-02 article 56(b) for a bank to send data to Argus, and would a bank partnership be outsourcing under Circular SB 011/12 and the 2025 operational-risk reglamento?
7. Is Argus a service provider that must keep access logs for 90 days under Ley 53-07 article 56?
8. Would a Hub de Innovación Financiera consultation produce a written position on read-only aggregation?
